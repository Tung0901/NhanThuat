# Personality Glossary

**Domain Area:** NT-DA-0003  
**Slug:** personality  
**Status:** frozen  

* **Adaptation cost:** The cognitive and physiological energy expended when an individual adapts their trait expression to meet situational or role demands that diverge from their defaults.
* **Behavioral default:** The automatic, low-effort response or action tendency an individual is most likely to express in the absence of strong situation-strength constraints or deliberate self-regulation.
* **Cognitive preference:** Relatively stable individual differences in how people gather, organize, process, and act on information (e.g., detail-oriented vs. big-picture, structured vs. emergent planning).
* **Compensatory strategy:** A learnable, conscious cognitive or behavioral technique used by an individual to bridge the gap between trait expression defaults and situational demands, mitigating adaptation cost.
* **Context sensitivity:** The degree to which an individual's trait expression is influenced, amplified, or suppressed by environmental cues and situational forces.
* **Masking:** The deliberate, high-effort, and resource-intensive suppression of one's natural personality defaults to conform to organizational, role, or social expectations over sustained periods.
* **Observed behavior:** The context-dependent, concrete action or response displayed by an individual, emerging dynamically from the interaction of trait expression, skills, motivation, experience, environment, and deliberate self-regulation.
* **Person-role fit:** A diagnostic, probabilistic, and hypothesis-generating assessment mapping the alignment between an individual's latent trait defaults and a role's demands. It is used to estimate potential adaptation costs rather than predict performance alone, and must never serve as an exclusion filter, hiring gate, or competence judgement.
* **Self-regulation:** The conscious cognitive control mechanism (executive control) used to manage trait expression and behavior. Its activation depends on cognitive load and situational constraints, without assuming a literal resource depletion (ego depletion) mechanism.
* **Situation strength:** The degree to which environmental demands, incentives, and rules clarify and enforce specific behaviors, where "strong situations" suppress trait differences and "weak situations" amplify trait expression.
* **Temperament:** The biologically rooted, early-appearing baseline differences in emotional reactivity, sensory sensitivity, activity level, and attentional control.
* **Trait:** A latent, relatively stable and enduring dimension of individual difference in patterns of thinking, feeling, or behaving. Traits act as baseline defaults but are adaptive, capable of gradual development, and not fixed.
* **Trait activation:** The process by which situational cues or environmental demands activate a latent trait, making its expression relevant to the context.
* **Trait expression:** The active interaction layer between latent traits and situation strength/context, representing the manifest tendency before it translates into final observed behavior.
