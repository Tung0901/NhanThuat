# Social Psychology Concept Map

**Domain Area:** NT-DA-0029  
**Slug:** social-psychology  
**Status:** review

## Core Question

How do group presence, norms, identity, and others' behavior change individual thought and action?

## Unit Inventory

*   **NT-MODEL-3301 — Social Identity Theory** (Model)
    - A person's sense of who they are is based on their group membership(s), driving ingroup favoritism and intergroup discrimination.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3301 — Social Proof and Conformity** (Phenomenon)
    - Individuals look to the behavior of others to determine the correct behavior for themselves, particularly in ambiguous situations.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3302 — Bystander Effect (Diffusion of Responsibility)** (Phenomenon)
    - The probability of an individual intervening in an emergency or taking ownership of a problem is inversely related to the number of people present.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3303 — Halo Effect** (Phenomenon)
    - The tendency for positive impressions of a person, company, brand or product in one area to positively influence one's opinion or feelings in other areas.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3304 — Groupthink** (Phenomenon)
    - A psychological phenomenon that occurs within a group of people in which the desire for harmony or conformity results in an irrational or dysfunctional decision-making outcome.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3305 — Ingroup Bias** (Phenomenon)
    - The tendency to favor one's own group over outgroup members.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3306 — Reciprocity Norm** (Phenomenon)
    - The powerful social rule that we should try to repay, in kind, what another person has provided us.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3307 — Social Loafing (Ringelmann Effect)** (Phenomenon)
    - The phenomenon of a person exerting less effort to achieve a goal when they work in a group than when they work alone.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3308 — Self-Fulfilling Prophecy (Pygmalion Effect)** (Phenomenon)
    - Higher expectations lead to an increase in performance, while lower expectations lead to a decrease.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3309 — Social Comparison Theory** (Phenomenon)
    - Individuals determine their own social and personal worth based on how they stack up against others.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3310 — Pluralistic Ignorance** (Phenomenon)
    - A situation where a majority of group members privately reject a norm, but incorrectly assume that most others accept it, and therefore go along with it.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3311 — Obedience to Authority** (Phenomenon)
    - The deep-seated tendency to comply with instructions from an authority figure, even when those instructions conflict with personal conscience.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3312 — Cognitive Dissonance** (Phenomenon)
    - The mental discomfort experienced by someone holding two or more contradictory beliefs, ideas, or values, especially when their behavior conflicts with their beliefs.
*   **NT-PRINCIPLE-3301 — Psychological Safety Design** (Principle)
    - Systematically removing the interpersonal risks of speaking up to prevent pluralistic ignorance and groupthink.
*   **NT-PRINCIPLE-3302 — Norm Activation (Descriptive vs Injunctive)** (Principle)
    - When trying to change behavior, never publicize that the bad behavior is widespread; instead, highlight the desired behavior as the social norm.
