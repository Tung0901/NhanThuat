# Cognitive Science & Sensemaking

**Domain ID:** NT-DA-0026
**Category:** CAT-BEHAVIORAL
**Status:** review

## 1. Scope
The study of how individuals and groups perceive, process, store, and act upon information. This domain covers the structural limits of attention, memory, and cognitive capacity, as well as the heuristics and models humans use to navigate ambiguity, uncertainty, and complexity.

## 2. Boundaries
- **In-Scope**: Cognitive load, attention allocation, memory reconstruction, sensemaking processes, heuristics, biases (anchoring, availability, confirmation, framing), metacognition, and ambiguity processing.
- **Out-of-Scope**: Emotional regulation (covered in Human Nature / Motivation), formal decision-making protocols (covered in Decision-Making), structural team dynamics (covered in Team Building).

## 3. Non-Goals
- We do not aim to catalogue every known cognitive bias in psychology.
- We do not reduce all human behavior to cognitive mechanical processes; emotion and identity remain distinct.
- We avoid prescribing rigid "de-biasing" programs; we focus on structural environment design.

## 4. Dependencies & Relationships
- **human-nature (NT-DA-0001)**: Provides the underlying biological and identity-driven constraints that shape cognitive resource allocation.
- **decision-making (NT-DA-0015)**: Depends heavily on Cognitive Science models to explain why formalized decision models often fail in practice.
- **learning (NT-DA-0018)**: Relies on Cognitive Load and Memory theories from this domain.
- **motivation (NT-DA-0002)**: Interacts with Attention and Metacognition (expectancy models).
- **cognitive-bias (NT-DA-0023)**: A sibling behavioral domain covering named cognitive biases as recurring patterns. Cognitive Science & Sensemaking treats biases as mechanisms of the broader sensemaking process; NT-DA-0023 documents the biases themselves. Both domains reference each other where units overlap.
