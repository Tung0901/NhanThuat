## 1. Domain Purpose

How do group presence, norms, identity, and others' behavior change individual thought and action?

## 2. Core Questions

*   Social Proof and Conformity: Individuals look to the behavior of others to determine the correct behavior for themselves, particularly in ambiguous situations.
*   Bystander Effect (Diffusion of Responsibility): The probability of an individual intervening in an emergency or taking ownership of a problem is inversely related to the number of people present.
*   Halo Effect: The tendency for positive impressions of a person, company, brand or product in one area to positively influence one's opinion or feelings in other areas.
*   Groupthink: A psychological phenomenon that occurs within a group of people in which the desire for harmony or conformity results in an irrational or dysfunctional decision-making outcome.
*   Ingroup Bias: The tendency to favor one's own group over outgroup members.
*   Reciprocity Norm: The powerful social rule that we should try to repay, in kind, what another person has provided us.
*   Social Loafing (Ringelmann Effect): The phenomenon of a person exerting less effort to achieve a goal when they work in a group than when they work alone.
*   Self-Fulfilling Prophecy (Pygmalion Effect): Higher expectations lead to an increase in performance, while lower expectations lead to a decrease.
*   Social Comparison Theory: Individuals determine their own social and personal worth based on how they stack up against others.
*   Pluralistic Ignorance: A situation where a majority of group members privately reject a norm, but incorrectly assume that most others accept it, and therefore go along with it.
*   Obedience to Authority: The deep-seated tendency to comply with instructions from an authority figure, even when those instructions conflict with personal conscience.
*   Cognitive Dissonance: The mental discomfort experienced by someone holding two or more contradictory beliefs, ideas, or values, especially when their behavior conflicts with their beliefs.
*   Psychological Safety Design: Systematically removing the interpersonal risks of speaking up to prevent pluralistic ignorance and groupthink.
*   Norm Activation (Descriptive vs Injunctive): When trying to change behavior, never publicize that the bad behavior is widespread; instead, highlight the desired behavior as the social norm.
*   Social Identity Theory: A person's sense of who they are is based on their group membership(s), driving ingroup favoritism and intergroup discrimination.
