# Persuasion & Influence Concept Map

**Domain Area:** NT-DA-0030  
**Slug:** persuasion-influence

## Core Question

How does influence actually move people, and where is the line between legitimate persuasion and manipulation?

## Unit Inventory

*   **NT-MODEL-3401 — Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM)** (Model)
    - A dual-process theory describing the change of attitudes, identifying two distinct routes to persuasion: central (logical) and peripheral (superficial).
*   **NT-MODEL-3402 — Inoculation Theory** (Model)
    - People can be immunized against persuasive attacks and misinformation by preemptively exposing them to a weakened version of the argument.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3401 — Scarcity Principle** (Phenomenon)
    - Opportunities seem more valuable to us when their availability is limited.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3402 — Authority Principle** (Phenomenon)
    - A deep-seated sense of duty to authority causes individuals to blindly comply with requests from perceived experts or leaders.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3403 — Liking Principle** (Phenomenon)
    - People prefer to say yes to individuals they know and like.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3404 — Commitment and Consistency** (Phenomenon)
    - Once we make a choice or take a stand, we face personal and interpersonal pressures to behave consistently with that commitment.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3405 — Unity Principle** (Phenomenon)
    - We are heavily influenced by people who share a fundamental identity with us—the 'We' connection.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3406 — Door-in-the-Face Technique** (Phenomenon)
    - Making an extreme request that is guaranteed to be rejected, followed immediately by a smaller, reasonable request.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3407 — Foot-in-the-Door Technique** (Phenomenon)
    - Getting a person to agree to a large request by first having them agree to a modest request.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3408 — Ben Franklin Effect** (Phenomenon)
    - A person who has performed a favor for someone is more likely to do another favor for them than they would be if they had received a favor from that person.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3409 — Identifiable Victim Effect** (Phenomenon)
    - People are far more willing to offer aid to a specific, identifiable individual in hardship than to a large, vaguely defined group with the same need.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3410 — Message Framing (Gain vs. Loss)** (Phenomenon)
    - The way a choice is presented (framed) as either a gain or a loss drastically alters the likelihood of persuasion, even if the objective outcomes are identical.
*   **NT-PHENOMENON-3411 — Paradox of Choice (Choice Overload)** (Phenomenon)
    - While some choice is better than none, an abundance of options leads to decision paralysis, anxiety, and lower post-decision satisfaction.
*   **NT-PRINCIPLE-3401 — Perceptual Contrast** (Principle)
    - Always present the most expensive, extreme, or difficult option first to alter the anchor point, making subsequent options appear significantly smaller or cheaper.
*   **NT-PRINCIPLE-3402 — Reason-Respecting Tendency ('Because' Heuristic)** (Principle)
    - People are highly likely to comply with a request if a reason is provided, even if that reason is completely meaningless.
